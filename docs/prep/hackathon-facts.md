# FestiveHacks: the facts (read from the official pages at 01:00 IST, 25 Nov 2026)

Sources: https://www.wemakedevs.org/hackathons/festivehacks (overview), /rules, /schedule, /resources.

## Event
- Organisers: WeMakeDevs × WeAreDevelopers Conference India. In person at BIEC, Bengaluru, on Wednesday 25 Nov 2026.
- Theme: "Build for the festive season." "Pick one real problem from those weeks, build a product that solves it, and show it working."
- No tracks. The suggested starting points include "Wedding season: RSVPs, Vendor tracking, Shagun ledger".

## Sponsors (their tools are required)
- **Flagsmith**: feature flags, remote config and A/B experimentation. Open source and self-hostable.
- **NudgeBee**: agentic ops / AI SRE for Kubernetes. Its Workflow Builder has HTTP tasks, approvals, CI/CD, Kubernetes and event tasks, and AI root-cause analysis.
- **Sarvam AI**: Indian-language AI, with speech-to-text (Saaras), text-to-speech (Bulbul), document intelligence, translation, LLMs (Token Factory, OpenAI-compatible) and voice agents.
- Overview prize box: "All projects must use all three sponsors' tools."
- Rules: "To win the trip to Berlin, a project must use all three sponsors' tools, and second place goes to the best use of Flagsmith."
- The resources page lists only the sponsors' docs, SDKs, blog posts and Discords, and has no "compulsory" wording of its own.

## Prizes
1. A fully sponsored trip to WeAreDevelopers World Congress 2027 in Berlin (July 2027). The project must use all three sponsors' tools.
2. Second place, "best use of Flagsmith": ₹1,00,000, Keychron keyboards and $1,000 in Sarvam AI credits.
3. Third place: Keychron keyboards and $1,000 in Sarvam AI credits.

## Judges
Kunal Kushwaha (founder, WeMakeDevs), Hitesh Choudhary (founder, Chai aur Code) and Saiyam Pathak (founder, Kubesimplify), plus about five more. **The judging criteria and their weights are not published.** "Judging decisions are final."

## Schedule (IST, 25 Nov)
- The rules give the hacking window as 9:00 AM–6:00 PM.
- 9:00–10:00 check-in.
- 10:00–11:30 keynote, opening conversation and sponsor demos (Flagsmith 11:00, NudgeBee 11:10, Sarvam 11:20).
- 11:30–1:00 hacking. 1:00–2:00 lunch. 2:00–4:15 hacking.
- 4:15–5:00 final build and submissions.
- 5:00–5:45 judging. 5:45–6:00 awards.
- **Submission deadline: 5:00 PM IST.** The overview and schedule say 5 PM; one line in the rules suggests 6 PM. Treat 5 PM as the deadline.

## Rules that constrain the build
- Teams of 1–4, and every member must be accepted and registered.
- "The main coding and design work must begin after the hackathon starts. Ideas, notes, sketches and research beforehand are fine."
- Frameworks, open-source libraries, public APIs and public datasets are allowed. "The work done during the hackathon is what gets judged."
- "AI coding assistants are allowed and their use must be disclosed. You have to understand the code you submit and be able to explain it."
- Submit a public repository, a README with setup instructions, and a demo video of the working project. "Something that runs, not slides." The demo video's length and format are not specified, and the submission platform is not named.
- Plagiarism or tampering with judging means disqualification.
